# WORKING-RULES: how the author works with the assistant

Written by Abdulsalam Abd. Quotes are his, verbatim. In these files "I", "me" and "my" are the author, Abdulsalam Abd, speaking to you, the assistant.

## Process rules

- M1. Structure by interrogation. The author's words: "I want every single idea, every single flow of the paper determined by
  me through questions, not by you. Understood? This is a hard rule." You ask many questions (10 to 50 is welcome),
  record each answer in substance, and assemble the structure only from my answers. You may offer options with a
  question. You recommend only when asked, except that every lettered option list has one RECOMMENDED (rules below).

- M2. Feasibility before manuscript. If the results are not yet assessed, write no manuscript prose. The author's words: "We
  will not write anything now. After we do proper outlines and discussion we need to start performing the real work
  to see if it is worth it. If it is, then we turn it into an article." Offering to "start drafting" is the wrong
  suggestion until I say the results are ready. Ask me whether this applies to this paper.

- M3. Disclosures: you draft, I approve. For a disclosure whose content I have already ruled, you draft at most five
  or six sentences in this register, and I approve each one at a time. The author's words: "You write them i approve them." A
  ten-sentence first draft was rejected ("redraft shorter"). Run the check before you ask him. Offer lettered options:
  approve, other section, edit, shorter. Do not name a dataset in a disclosure sentence unless I say so.

- M11. Do not stop work because I mention cost or usage. A remark is information, not a stop order. Stop only when I
  say stop.

- M12. Briefs and notes for sub-agents or later sessions are written to files in the project, not given inline, and
  they carry an explicit "do not spawn further agents" line.

## How to ask the author questions

These rules come from how the author works. Follow them for every decision in this project, not only at setup.

- One decision per question. Put 3 to 6 sentences of plain context inside the question itself, because text before a
  question is lost when the question appears.
- Give lettered options. Exactly one option is marked RECOMMENDED, with its evidence in one line. I answer with a
  letter. Batches only if I ask for them.
- If my reply is not a clean pick (a comment or a question), answer that alone and stop. Do not open the next question
  in the same turn. If I ask a follow-up, the answer goes at the top of the next question text ("ANSWER FIRST").
- After an answered question, record it and ask the next one at once. No "say go" pauses.
- Use simple words. Jargon gets a plain gloss. Short sentences.
- Do not take a decision for me that is mine (paper structure, claims, what to disclose). If I have granted you a
  stretch of autonomy in writing ("auto-run"), take the decision provisionally, label it PROVISIONAL in a list of
  pending ratifications, and keep going without asking.

## Figures and tables

- Simple beats complete, always (my ruling, twice, emphatically). Main figures show only the fitted or claimed
  domain. No other series beyond the claim, no flagged-outlier markers, no deviation strips. Per-panel numbers go in
  the caption. Everything cut from a figure goes into one compact table in the supporting information; nothing is
  dropped silently. Guardrail I accepted: within a figure's stated scope, every qualifying measurement stays, and
  in-scope points are never thinned. Captions declare the scope in one phrase.
- Keep the agreed look. When a figure is regenerated, start from the agreed scripts and keep layout, sizes, fonts,
  colours, markers and legends; change only what a named fix needs. My reaction to a fresh-styled set: "horrible".
  Every figure brief names the reference figure set and requires a side-by-side check that lists each visual
  difference and the fix that caused it.
- Template I approved (print size): full width 175 mm, column width 85 mm with panels stacked; 8 pt axis text; source
  keys inside the panels' empty space without frames (about 7 pt); one colour key for temperature at the bottom; "open
  symbols = experimental data". ALWAYS symbols on axes ("x_CO2", "y_H2O", "P / MPa"), with short panel titles ("CO2,
  liquid"). My words: "its much better now, always use symbols". Ratio axes are spelled out in words with the symbol
  after ("brine / pure-water solubility, m/m0"). Every figure build runs an automatic overlap check (text, legends,
  insets, tick labels, keys against data) and fails on any collision.
- Do not make figures large. My comments: "fig 6 7 8 are large to. the feel in you r face when u open the apaper";
  make figures of the same kind the same size.
- Captions are short: a title fragment, a symbol key in "Symbol: meaning." form, and at most one sentence on anything
  unusual ("short-plus-one"). No deviation numbers or point counts in captions when the table has them. A caption that
  gets long is shortened.
- Tables: the columns of tables of the same kind are the same (a column in one leg's table that the other leg lacks is
  either justified or removed). Justify every column or drop it. Explain a symbol such as "+" in a count, or make the
  table clear without it. Leave a small vertical gap above a Total row. Table captions define the column basis;
  lettered footnotes carry exceptions.
- Line numbers on in both the submission version and the normal version of the manuscript.
